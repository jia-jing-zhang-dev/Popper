Retrieval Practice at Scale: Spacing, Feedback,
and Long-Term Vocabulary Retention

Lin Zhou  ·  Maya Patel  ·  Daniel Okafor
Department of Computer Science and Learning Sciences Lab
ABSTRACT Second-language readers meet unfamiliar words constantly, yet looking a word up rarely leads to remembering it. We study 41,000 learners who looked words up while reading and then reviewed them on an expanding schedule. Spaced retrieval with immediate feedback is ubiquitous in the literature but is seldom measured in the wild; here it roughly doubles thirty-day retention relative to massed review, and the benefit is largest for words first met in context. We describe a lightweight model of memory consolidation that predicts when each word should return.

1  Introduction

Reading in a second language is interrupted by unknown words. A dictionary lookup resolves the immediate problem, but without later retrieval the meaning fades within days. The spacing effect, first described over a century ago, suggests that reviews separated by growing intervals strengthen memory far more than the same number of reviews in a row.

Prior studies are mostly laboratory experiments with fixed word lists. Learners in practice are heterogeneous: they read different texts, look up different words and return on irregular days.

2  Method

Each lookup creates a card. A card returns after 1, 2, 4, 7, 15, 30 and 60 days while it is answered correctly, and after one day when it is forgotten.
